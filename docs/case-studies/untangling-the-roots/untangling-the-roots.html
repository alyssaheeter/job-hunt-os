Untangling the Roots

Turning a 329-person Ancestry export into a verified, people-first knowledge graph.

I built a validated graph from a raw family-tree export, then used scripts to find 30 ancestors the tree had silently lost. My first diagnosis was wrong, and the data corrected it.

The problem

An Ancestry export is a flat file, not a research tool. Imported as-is, citations, places and events outnumbered people, so the graph showed hubs instead of a family. I wanted a graph with only people and kinship edges, every person labelled by relationship, and proof by script that the data could be trusted.

The bug: ancestors who "weren't related to anyone"

Thirty people had no path to the root, in two tidy clusters of 13 and 17. My theory was a missing marriage joining each cluster to the main tree. I wrote a script to test it, and the result was the opposite:

  • The source file was fully connected: 329 of 329 people reached the root.
  • Tracing paths showed both clusters were direct ancestors, five to six generations up.
  • Two people in the chain were duplicate records. Ancestry stored each twice, once with parents and once without, and my vault linked to the copy without parents.

The lesson: a tag like unlinked can be right about the database and wrong about the family. I only found that by checking against an independent source.

Judgement calls I did not automate

  • Candidate 1: same name, one-year birth gap. A strong match and a likely merge.
  • Candidate 2: same name, a 32-year birth gap. A real conflict, flagged for a record check rather than guessed.

The script writes these to a review queue and never edits notes. Automation finds candidates, and a human decides.

Results

  • 329 people validated against 100 family records.
  • 30 hidden ancestors diagnosed, with a root cause and merge plan.
  • A repeatable --verify pipeline that keeps the graph people-only.
  • Two family-facing web portals shipped.
